How to read the results: win % is the model's chance for each side, and fair odds are what that chance is worth with no sportsbook cut. Player props are harder to predict than game lines: in testing, this model beat a plain season average by only 1–3%, so treat big edges with suspicion and check the news.

The model's projection and fair line for every prop. Compare the fair line with your sportsbook's: if theirs is lower, the over looks better; if higher, the under.

Each player's projection starts from his per-game average, with recent games counting more (each older game counts 0.9 as much as the one after it) and last season's games at 30% weight. Passing props are adjusted for the opponent's pass defense. Touchdown props are scaled by how many points the team ratings project the team to score, compared with its usual average. Rushing and receiving props aren't adjusted for the opponent, because in testing that made them less accurate.

Results vary around the projection by amounts measured from the 2024 and 2025 seasons: yards follow a skewed (lognormal) curve, touchdowns a Poisson distribution, and other counts a negative binomial. The model doesn't know about injuries to teammates, depth-chart changes or weather. Sportsbooks usually void a prop if the player doesn't play.
